\subsection{Independent exact algorithms}
\label{subsec:aor-exact-algorithms}

The tagged representation gives two independent exact finite methods.  They
share only validated input, mandatory-selection preprocessing, and the final
source-library certificate; their search procedures are different.  Complete
enumeration is the small-library oracle.  Requirement-mask dynamic programming
is parameterized by the number of residual tagged requirements.

Fix every mandatory strategy first.  Let $W_0$ be their exact burden, delete
every requirement they satisfy, and let the residual instance have strategies
$1,\ldots,n$ and requirements $1,\ldots,r$.  Write $R_i\subseteq[r]$ for
the residual tagged coverage of strategy $i$ and $w_i>0$ for its exact weight.
Mandatory preprocessing alone removes no optional strategy, so it preserves
all optimizer identities.  If a serialized instance already contains a valid
stronger preprocessing map, the algorithms use its residual model, exact
offset, and declared reconstruction map; complete-tie output is rejected when
that map says that some equal-cost identities are unreconstructable.

\subsubsection{Feasibility-safe preprocessing core}

\begin{proposition}[Finite covering identities used in preprocessing]
\label{prop:aor-preprocessing-feasibility-core}
For a finite residual pure covering model: every feasible selection contains
the unique carrier of a required row; replacing a selected column by a column
whose residual coverage contains it preserves feasibility; a nonmandatory
column covering no residual requirement may be erased; and one of two
requirement rows with identical carrier predicates may be erased without
changing the feasible selections.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unique-carrier statement follows directly from the covering inequality.
For dominance, redirect any row witnessed by the replaced column to the
coverage superset.  An empty-contribution column witnesses no residual row,
so erasing it preserves every existing witness.  Identical carrier predicates
make the two covering inequalities logically identical.  These arguments are
finite-set feasibility identities.  Weight inequalities, objective offsets,
optimizer identities, reconstruction maps, and fixed-point termination require
the additional propositions and proofs in the preprocessing report.
\end{proof}

The four feasibility clauses above are Lean verified.  They are supporting
lemmas, not a Lean proof of the complete preprocessing pipeline: optimum-value
preservation, tie reconstruction, propagation, and fixed-point termination
remain human proofs with exhaustive exact Julia tests.
The shared finite incidence fixture has three required rows and cover sets
\(R_0=\{0\}\), \(R_1=\{1,2\}\), and \(R_2=\{2\}\); its Lean declaration
checks that row zero has unique carrier zero and hence every complete cover
selects strategy zero.  This is one exact instance of the first clause, not a
replacement for the universal proposition.

\subsubsection{Complete enumeration}
The enumeration method visits every one of the $2^n$ residual binary
selections.  It tests tagged coverage directly, sums burden in exact rational
arithmetic, and retains either every minimum or the declared stable
representative.  It then lifts forced selections and rechecks mandatory
retention, the original operating frontier, the original identity closure, and
the exact burden independently of the search test.

\begin{proposition}[Correctness of complete enumeration]
\label{prop:aor-complete-enumeration}
On a valid finite tagged identity-closure instance, complete enumeration
returns a minimum-burden safe source sublibrary.  When complete-tie retention
is requested and the preprocessing map preserves all optimizer identities, it
returns every minimum-burden source selection.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under mandatory-only preprocessing, every admissible source sublibrary is the
forced set together with exactly one of the $2^n$ residual subsets.
Enumeration evaluates all of them.  Its feasibility test is therefore true
for precisely the feasible residual subsets, and its exact minimum is the
residual optimum.  Adding the selection-independent burden $W_0$ preserves
objective order, and lifting is a feasibility- and burden-preserving
bijection.

If the input already carries stronger preprocessing, the preprocessing
propositions establish optimum-value preservation and a valid canonical lift;
the solver does not reassert a bijection for eliminated columns.  When the map
also declares all optimizer identities reconstructable, its equal-coverage
classes enumerate the additional optimal identities.  Finally, the
independent original-library checks exclude a corrupted lift or incidence-only
certificate.  The tagged-cover equivalence theorem identifies every checked
lift with a safe source sublibrary.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Requirement-mask dynamic program}
Encode a requirement subset by an $r$-bit mask; union is bitwise OR.  The
implemented forward recurrence is the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Set $D_0[0]=0$ and leave every other mask unreachable.
\item For $i=1,\ldots,n$ and every mask $q$, carry the skip transition
      $(q,D_{i-1}[q])$ and the take transition
      $(q\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}R_i,D_{i-1}[q]+w_i)$ into layer $i$, keeping
      the lower exact burden.
\item At equal burden, keep the first predecessor under the declared stable
      traversal, or retain every tied predecessor when complete-tie mode is
      requested.
\item Return the entry for the all-ones mask, add $W_0$, reconstruct the
      original selection, and run the independent source-library certificate.
\end{enumerate}

The implementation scans masks in increasing integer order and strategies in
canonical identifier order, emitting the skip transition before the take
transition.  In representative mode it keeps the first exact predecessor; in
complete-tie mode it stores the complete optimal predecessor DAG and expands
it only during reconstruction.  Thus iteration and representative tie
selection are deterministic; hash-table iteration order does not determine
output.

The shared finite fixture checks the take-transition identity
\([1,0,1]\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}[0,1,0]=[1,1,1]\) in both Julia and Lean.
That calculation validates one mask union only; it is not a Lean proof of the
inductive optimum invariant, reconstruction, complexity, or FPT statement.

\begin{theorem}[Exact requirement-mask dynamic programming]
\label{thm:aor-requirement-mask-dp}
For a valid finite tagged identity-closure instance, the recurrence above
returns a minimum-burden safe source sublibrary and reconstructs at least one
optimizer.  In representative mode it uses $O(n2^r)$ exact-arithmetic and mask
operations and $O(2^r)$ cost states plus $O(n2^r)$ reconstruction-aware
predecessor records.  If only optimum value were required, the two cost layers
would use $O(2^r)$ memory.  Complete-tie retention is output-sensitive:
materializing $T$ tied reconstruction vectors adds $O(Tn)$ bits and can be
exponential in $n$.

Consequently, the representative algorithm is fixed-parameter tractable in
$r$: its bit-complexity is $2^r$ times a polynomial in the explicit input
length.  It is not polynomial in the full input size when $r$ is unrestricted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}$ and mask $q$, let $D_i[q]$ be the minimum exact
burden of a subset of the first $i$ residual strategies whose coverage union
is exactly $q$, with $D_i[q]=+\infty$ if no such subset exists.  We prove the
stored invariant by induction on $i$.  At $i=0$, only the empty subset exists,
so $D_0[0]=0$ and all other states are unreachable.

Assume the invariant at $i-1$.  Every subset of the first $i$ strategies
either omits strategy $i$, in which case it supplies the skip candidate already
characterized by $D_{i-1}$, or contains strategy $i$.  Removing $i$ from the
latter subset leaves some predecessor mask $q$ with optimal predecessor cost
$D_{i-1}[q]$; reinserting it produces mask $q\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}R_i$ and
cost $D_{i-1}[q]+w_i$.  Conversely, every skip or take transition constructs
a subset of the first $i$ strategies with the stated mask and burden.  Taking
the minimum over these exhaustive and disjoint cases proves the invariant for
layer $i$.  Stored predecessor records follow the same transitions, so every
finite state has a valid path to the base layer.  Equal-cost merging preserves
the first predecessor under the declared deterministic traversal; retaining
every equal-cost predecessor preserves the complete optimal predecessor DAG.
Expanding that DAG preserves every tied residual identity, subject to the
explicitly enforced output cap.

At $i=n$, a residual selection is feasible exactly when its union is the
all-ones mask.  Hence $D_n[2^r-1]$ is the residual optimum.  Adding the fixed
mandatory offset and lifting preserve objective value directly under
mandatory-only preprocessing; for a supplied stronger map, those properties
are the content of the preprocessing propositions.  Applying the tagged-cover
equivalence proves safe-compression optimality.  The independent exact
certificate rechecks the source frontier, source closure, mandatory
strategies, and burden rather than using the DP mask as its sole evidence.

There are $2^r$ masks and two relaxations per strategy-mask pair, giving
$O(n2^r)$ recurrence operations in the unit-cost mask/arithmetic model.  Two
layers contain at most $2^r$ exact costs.  The implementation additionally
stores optimal predecessor records across the $n$ layers, giving the stated
reconstruction-aware bound.  Exact rational sums and comparisons have bit
cost polynomial in the explicit input bit length: sums of at most $n$ encoded
weights have polynomially bounded numerator and denominator length.  Thus the
bit-time bound is $2^r\operatorname{poly}(|I|)$, which is fixed-parameter
tractable in $r$ and remains exponential when $r$ is part of unrestricted
input.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Evidence boundary and counters}
Both methods are exact finite computations, not Lean kernel verification and
not mixed-integer solver evidence.  The supporting mask-union declaration
does not change that status.  The standard result object records the
exact objective and checked selections separately from wall-clock
measurements.  For enumeration, candidate count is the number of binary
subsets tested.  For the DP, a visited state is a reachable mask at one
strategy layer and a transition is one evaluated skip or take relaxation;
the final reachable-state count is reported separately.  Runtime fields are
single-run measurements split into preprocessing, search, and
reconstruction/certification scopes.  They are instrumentation, not an
algorithmic scaling finding; scaling evidence requires the separately locked
benchmark design.
