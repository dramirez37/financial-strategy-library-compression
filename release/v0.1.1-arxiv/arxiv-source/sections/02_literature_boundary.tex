\section{Literature boundary}\label{sec:literature-boundary}

The paper draws on established ideas from partial-information and semi-Markov
control, state and policy reduction, compact control libraries, generative
action sets, evolving skill libraries, and automated financial-strategy
construction.  We treat each of those ingredients as prior art.  The claimed
boundary is narrower: their joint use in a finite model where a retained
strategy can be both a current control and a carrier needed to generate later
verified descendants.  This section first locates the broad ingredients and
then concentrates on the closest compression and library comparisons.

\subsection{Control and representation}

Belief-state reduction is standard in partially observable Markov decision
processes.  Finite-horizon POMDP values admit piecewise-linear convex
representations, and incremental pruning removes redundant alpha vectors from
fixed-model dynamic-programming backups
\cite{SmallwoodSondik1973,CassandraLittmanZhang1997}.  Bisimulation and related
equivalence relations support state aggregation and model minimization when
states agree on the behavior relevant to a specified comparison class
\cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}.  Semi-Markov control likewise accommodates random
decision durations.  White develops a sufficient-statistic dynamic program
for a finite-horizon, partially observed semi-Markov problem
\cite{White1976}; partial-information switching and regime-switching trading
models use filtered beliefs to govern costly changes of operating mode
\cite{LiNystromOlofsson2015,DaiZhangZhu2010}.

These results supply the control architecture used here.  The paper's
frontier--closure statistic is a model-specific reduction of an augmented raw
process, not a new general theory of POMDP sufficiency or semi-Markov timing.
The additional issue is that the library is endogenous state: two libraries
can have the same current payoff envelope but different parent-conditioned
candidate laws.  The operating frontier records current control value, while
the generative closure records the modules available to the modeled research
process.  Standard alpha-vector pruning or MDP aggregation could be applied to
a fully encoded augmented model; the present reduction identifies the smaller
statistic implied by the paper's finite generation, verification, and
admission law.

Safety and statistical validation provide other preservation criteria.
Finite-state-controller methods give high-probability policy improvement for
POMDPs, while static policy libraries can support finite-horizon runtime-safety
coverage \cite{SimaoSuilenJansen2023,KimEtAl2026PLCBF}.  Reality-check and
high-confidence policy-improvement methods address selection bias or the
performance of evaluated policies
\cite{White2000,ThomasTheocharousGhavamzadeh2015}.  Those criteria remain
important but distinct from exact equality of a source library's current
frontier and attainable descendant closure.

\subsection{Compression and resource-limited libraries}

\paragraph{Reward-free policy-space compression.}
Mutti, Del Col, and Restelli compress a parametric policy space into finitely
many representatives whose induced state--action distributions provide
reward-free R\'enyi-divergence coverage.  Their formulation is related to set
cover and is NP-hard in general \cite{MuttiDelColRestelli2022}.  This is the
closest policy-compression comparison because it selects a compact policy set
before downstream rewards are specified.  Its preserved object is
distributional representativeness.  Here the feasible sets are sublibraries
of a named source, and feasibility requires exact equality of two semantic
objects.  A currently dominated strategy can therefore be retained when it is
the unique carrier of a module required by an attainable descendant.  The two
problems impose different covering relations even though both value compact
policy sets.

\paragraph{Control-library optimization.}
Dey et al. select and order compact libraries of maneuvers or trajectories,
using submodular-sequence structure to improve expected task performance or
reduce search depth \cite{DeyEtAl2012}.  This is the closest classical
control-library optimization problem: the library itself is the decision
variable and computation is scarce.  Its candidates are evaluated through
static task usefulness.  In the present model, retaining a candidate can also
change the future candidate set, so module complementarities can matter even
when current operating value does not.  Consequently, exact safe compression
is a finite combinatorial minimization under frontier--closure equality;
rechecked single deletions certify feasibility but do not generally solve the
global minimum-burden problem.

Metareasoning prices computations by their effect on later decisions, and
adaptive-submodular formulations obtain greedy guarantees under diminishing
stochastic marginal benefits
\cite{RussellWefald1991,HayEtAl2012,GolovinKrause2011}.  These are the natural
resource-allocation antecedents.  The paper does not assume the diminishing
returns needed for a generic greedy guarantee.  Its hard-capacity and
resource-price variants instead optimize over finitely many eligible
libraries; complementarities and discrete changes in the selected library
can produce nonconcave capacity increments and piecewise-affine penalized
value.

\paragraph{Generative action sets.}
Xu et al. study online decision making when an agent can pay once to generate
a new action that remains available, and derive a sublinear-regret algorithm
for the creation--exploration--exploitation trade-off
\cite{XuEtAl2026GenerativeActions}.  This establishes costly generation and
persistent reuse as prior art.  In that model, action generation expands the
menu but is not conditioned on retaining particular existing actions as
parents.  Here that dependency changes the deletion problem: removal can
leave every current action value unchanged while removing the modules needed
to reach a later action.  The distinction is parent-conditioned reachability,
not the broad idea that actions can be generated.

\paragraph{Internal procedural compression.}
Recent agent-skill compression methods reduce internal procedural
representations.  One SkillZip system rewrites recurring motifs in
section-level execution graphs into reversible macros while preserving
boundary contracts, dependency closure, verifier reachability, and source
expansion \cite{TanEtAl2026SkillZipGraph}.  A separate SkillZip system seeks a
typed minimum-description-length representation subject to hard coverage of
every extracted trigger, workflow edge, tool requirement, obligation, and
output field \cite{BaiEtAl2026SkillZipMDL}.  These methods preserve executable
structure within skill artifacts.  The present decision variable is instead a
sublibrary of whole entries in a named financial-strategy library.  Its exact
feasibility condition is equality of the belief-indexed operating frontier
and generative closure, which preserves both current control opportunities
and the modeled parent-conditioned process by which later strategies can be
generated and admitted.

\subsection{Maintained and evolving skill libraries}

Maintained skill libraries make parent and dependency structure operational.
As motivation for treating growth as an operational burden, Song and Wei
document performance degradation from skill shadowing as libraries expand
\cite{SongWei2026SkillShadowing}.
SkillFoundry builds validated executable skills from heterogeneous scientific
resources and expands, repairs, merges, or prunes the resulting library
\cite{ShenEtAl2026SkillFoundry}.  SkillOps represents skills by typed contracts
and organizes dependency, compatibility, validation, and maintenance signals
in a library-level ecosystem graph \cite{PuSongZhao2026SkillOps}.
Programmatic Skill Networks link executable programs through invocations and
use duplicate removal, link redirection, branch pruning, and rollback
validation during evolution \cite{ShiYuanLiu2026PSN}.  These systems are the
closest dependency-aware comparisons because removing one entry can affect
later composition, retrieval, or construction rather than only present task
performance.

Related systems recursively evolve skill banks, maintain typed skill graphs,
construct hierarchical or versioned registries, compile external knowledge
into reusable procedures, and synthesize code policies from validated
components
\cite{XiaEtAl2026SkillRL,LiEtAl2026SkillGraph,WangEtAl2026SkillX,%
PanEtAl2026Anything2Skill,ChunEtAl2026FCGraft}.  Together they establish
maintained, evolving, compositional skill libraries as prior art.  Their
maintenance objectives are benchmark success, retrieval quality, validation,
latency, compatibility, or lifecycle health.  The present abstraction takes a
fixed finite generation--verification--admission law as primitive and asks a
different audit question: which source entries can be removed while exactly
preserving both the operating frontier and every modeled feasible admitted
descendant?  It is not a replacement for engineering criteria concerning
tests, provenance, repair, retrieval, or rollback.

\subsection{Generated financial strategies}

Automated financial-rule construction also has a long history.  Genetic
search has been used to generate technical trading rules, and direct
reinforcement learning to construct trading policies
\cite{AllenKarjalainen1999,MoodySaffell2001}.  More recent systems add
persistent libraries, reusable experience, and verification.  FactorMiner
combines formulaic factors with reusable evaluation skills and experience
memory; ReCAP maintains regime-specific portfolio policies and combines them
through a regime gate
\cite{WangEtAl2026FactorMiner,PanEtAl2026ReCAP}.  These are substantive
financial library precedents: one uses retained experience to guide later
generation, while the other maintains a regime-specific operating policy
library.

AlgoEvolve and EVOQUANT are the closest generated-strategy systems.
AlgoEvolve evolves executable trading programs and uses selected programs as
parents of later mutations; EVOQUANT applies controlled edits, provenance,
verification gates, and retained optimization experience
\cite{SharmaShroff2026AlgoEvolve,MaoEtAl2026EvoQuant}.  They demonstrate that
financial strategies can be generated, empirically screened, retained, and
used in later search.  The present paper begins after the finite generator and
admission law have been specified and studies source-relative retention under
partial information.  Its retrospective financial audits are therefore
compression diagnostics, not evidence of causal improvement, forecasting
ability, market alpha, or deployable performance.

\paragraph{Joint claim boundary.}
This paper does not claim novelty for belief-state or semi-Markov control,
policy or control-library compression, generated actions, evolving skill
libraries, or automated financial-strategy generation.  Its claim concerns
the joint finite-model treatment.  The raw partial-information process
projects to belief, operating frontier, and generative closure; exact
source-relative compression then minimizes retention burden while preserving
both productive objects and the modeled control process.  Rechecked
frontier--closure equality certifies sequential deletion, although a
deletion-irreducible endpoint need not be globally minimum-resource, and a
sharp bridge construction shows that frontier-only pruning can lose the
complete attainable delayed-descendant margin.  Over finite eligible
libraries, hard-capacity and resource-price selection yield attained but
potentially nonconcave, complementary, and discrete geometry, while productive
value separates operational and generative channels within fixed regimes.
Exact fixtures, registered synthetic stress tests, and retrospective financial
audits examine these mechanisms at distinct evidence levels.  The claimed
boundary is this package as a unit, not any ingredient in isolation.
