\section{Exact compression, covering representation, and complexity}
\label{sec:cover-complexity}
\label{sec:compression}

Fix a verified finite source library \(L\).  Safety is source-relative:
compression may delete source entries but may neither import a replacement nor
change the declared profiles, modules, or closure operator.

\subsection{Safe feasibility and productive preservation}

The safe-feasible family and its global minimum-weight correspondence are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{safe}}(L)
   &:=\{L'\subseteq L:s_0\in L',\
          F_{L'}=F_L,\ C_{L'}=C_L\},
   \label{eq:safe-compression-family}\\
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{safe}}(L)
   &:=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{L'\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{safe}}(L)}W(L').
   \label{eq:minimum-safe-compression}
\end{align}
Equality is exact at every declared belief and for the entire declared
generative closure.  In particular, a currently dominated strategy is not
safe to remove merely because it contributes no current frontier value.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum-resource innovation-safe representation]
\label{thm:minimum-safe-compression}
Let \(L\) be a finite source library containing \(s_0\), let the catalog and
closure satisfy \cref{asm:aor-projection-process}, and let
\(w_{s_0}=0\) with exact rational \(w_s>0\) for every active source strategy.
Then \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{safe}}(L)\) is finite and nonempty,
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{safe}}(L)\neq\varnothing\), and every
\(L'\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{safe}}(L)\) has the same finite-horizon productive
values as \(L\).  Under the contraction clause of
\cref{asm:aor-projection-process}, it also has the same stationary productive
value and complete optimal stationary-action correspondence.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The source is feasible relative to itself and has only finitely many
sublibraries, so an exact burden minimum is attained.  Feasibility is exactly
\(K_{L'}=K_L\); the value and action conclusions follow from
\cref{thm:raw-to-compressed-projection}.  Neither optimizer uniqueness nor a
particular deletion path follows.

A converse from equal observed behavior to equal closure requires an additional detectability assumption; the supporting proof states that separate condition.

\subsection{Identity closure induces a tagged cover}
\label{subsec:aor-tagged-cover}

The reduction in this subsection is the computational consequence of the
preservation semantics. Once the tagged universe is constructed, the resulting
optimization problem is ordinary weighted set cover: its generic binary model,
NP-hardness antecedent, greedy approximation theorem, mask recurrence, and
standard cover preprocessing are not claimed as new combinatorial-optimization
theory. The application-specific content is the exact mapping from frontier and
closure equality to tagged coverage, together with its identity-closure limit.

The algorithms below apply to identity closure. General closure remains governed by exact equality of the closed module unions.


Assume throughout this subsection that closure is identity.  Define the
disjoint tagged requirement universe
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_L^{\mathrm{tag}}
 :=\{\mathsf{frontier}(b):b\in B\}
   \mathbin{\dot\cup}
   \{\mathsf{module}(m):m\in C_L\},
 \label{eq:tagged-requirement-universe}
\end{equation}
and, for each \(s\in L\), its source-relative coverage
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_s
 :=\{\mathsf{frontier}(b):j_s(b)=F_L(b)\}
   \cup
   \{\mathsf{module}(m):m\in\mods(s)\}.
 \label{eq:strategy-tagged-coverage}
\end{equation}
Frontier and module tags remain distinct even when their underlying identifiers
print alike.  Exact equality in the first term means that every tied source
attainer covers the belief row.

\begin{theorem}[Tagged-cover equivalence under identity closure]
\label{thm:aor-tagged-cover-equivalence}
Let \(B,M,S\) be finite, let \(B\neq\varnothing\), and let
\(s_0\in L'\subseteq L\subseteq S\).  Profiles are exact rational functions
\(j_s:B\to\mathbb Q\); \(j_{s_0}=0\), \(\mods(s_0)=\varnothing\), maxima are
attained, and closure is identity on \(2^M\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 F_{L'}=F_L\ \text{and}\ C_{L'}=C_L
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal U_L^{\mathrm{tag}}\subseteq
       \bigcup_{s\in L'}\mathcal R_s.
 \label{eq:tagged-cover-equivalence}
\end{equation}
No unique frontier attainer or unique module carrier is assumed.  If
\(F_L(b)=0\), mandatory \(s_0\) itself covers
\(\mathsf{frontier}(b)\).
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
If \(L'\) preserves the frontier, a finite maximizer in \(L'\) covers each
frontier tag.  Under identity closure,
\(C_{L'}=C_L\) is equality of raw module unions, so some retained carrier
covers every module tag.  Conversely, source inclusion gives
\(F_{L'}\leq F_L\) and \(C_{L'}\subseteq C_L\).  Coverage supplies a retained
source attainer at every belief and a retained raw carrier for every source
module, giving the reverse inclusions.  Ties and multiple carriers are
existential choices, while the zero-frontier row is witnessed by \(s_0\).

For exact rational weights, the equivalent binary model is
\begin{align}
 \min_x\quad &\sum_{s\in L}w_sx_s,
     \label{eq:tagged-cover-objective}\\
 \text{s.t.}\quad
 &\sum_{s\in L:\,j_s(b)=F_L(b)}x_s\geq1,
       &&b\in B,
       \label{eq:tagged-frontier-rows}\\
 &\sum_{s\in L:\,m\in\mods(s)}x_s\geq1,
       &&m\in C_L,
       \label{eq:tagged-module-rows}\\
 &x_{s_0}=1,\qquad x_s\in\{0,1\},
       &&s\in L.
       \label{eq:tagged-binary-domain}
\end{align}
The source restriction is built into the columns.  All zero-frontier rows may
be removed after fixing \(s_0\), but retaining them in
\(\mathcal U_L^{\mathrm{tag}}\) keeps the semantic construction literal.

The boundary is substantive.  If
\(\cl(\{a,b\})=\{a,b,c\}\) although no strategy carries \(c\), an independent
row for each member of \(C_L\) rejects even the source library.  General
closure therefore requires direct closure equality or a separately proved
extended formulation.  Frontier rows alone and module rows alone are likewise
insufficient; exact counterexamples are in Online Resource~1, Section~S3.

\subsection{Baseline mixed-integer formulation}
\label{subsec:aor-baseline-mip}

Equations~\eqref{eq:tagged-cover-objective}--\eqref{eq:tagged-binary-domain}
are the baseline MIP.  Its input is a validated, canonically ordered
identity-closure instance with exact weights and declared mandatory columns.
Exact preprocessing may fix columns and remove satisfied rows before JuMP and
HiGHS receive the residual binary cover.  Exact weights are scaled to an
integer objective only after the scaling identity and solver range are
verified.

Each returned selection is lifted to original strategy identities and independently
checked for mandatory retention, frontier equality, closure equality, and exact
burden. Fractional vectors are rejected without rounding. Solver status and
bounds remain numerical evidence of global optimality; the exact postcheck
certifies the returned library's feasibility and burden. If preprocessing
solves the instance completely, no solver is invoked.

\subsection{Computational complexity}
\label{subsec:aor-safe-compression-complexity}

\textsc{Identity-Closure Safe Compression} (\textsc{ISC-ID}) has the following
explicit decision encoding.  The input lists a finite source
\(L=\{s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_n\}\), a finite nonempty belief set, exact
binary-rational profiles, a finite module universe and incidence rows,
identity closure, positive binary-integer active weights, and a nonnegative
binary-integer threshold \(D\).  Strategy \(s_0\) is mandatory, has zero
profile, no modules, and zero weight.  The question is whether some
\(s_0\in L'\subseteq L\) preserves the exact source frontier and closure and
has \(W(L')\leq D\).

\begin{theorem}[Identity-closure safe-compression complexity]
\label{thm:aor-safe-compression-complexity}
Under the explicit encoding in the preceding paragraph,
\textnormal{\textsc{ISC-ID}} is NP-complete.  It remains NP-complete on the
restricted subclass with one belief, zero profiles for every strategy, a
zero-weight module-free inactive strategy, identity closure, a source whose
active module rows have full union, and unit active weights.  Consequently,
minimum-burden identity-closure safe compression is NP-hard, and any broader
model class containing this subclass is NP-hard.  No NP-membership conclusion
for arbitrary nonidentity closure follows unless its encoding admits a
polynomial exact closure-equality verifier.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.}
A selected-strategy bit vector is a polynomial certificate: exact rational
maxima, row unions, binary-integer burden, source inclusion, and mandatory
retention are polynomially checkable in the explicit input length.  For
hardness, normalize Karp's \textsc{Set Cover} decision problem
\citep{Karp1972} to a nonempty universe and a full-union indexed family, using
fixed yes/no outputs for the empty- or uncovered-universe branches.  Give each
universe element a module, each indexed set an active strategy carrying its
modules, every active strategy unit weight and zero profile, and add one
zero-profile inactive strategy.  Use one belief and copy the budget.  The
inactive strategy preserves the trivial frontier, while identity-closure
equality is exactly set-cover equality and burden is exactly selected-set
cardinality.  Incidence size and integer bit lengths are copied polynomially.
The complete membership, normalization, forward, reverse, size, and threshold
audit is in Online Resource~1, Section~S3.  No strong-hardness,
inapproximability, or APX-hardness claim is made.

\subsection{Exact preprocessing of the tagged cover}
\label{subsec:aor-preprocessing}

Fix the tagged universe from the source before reducing the model.  After
declared mandatory strategies are selected, let \(Q\) be the unsatisfied rows,
\(A\) the available nonfixed columns, and
\(\mathcal R_s^Q=\mathcal R_s\cap Q\).  Mandatory weights are nonnegative,
optional weights are strictly positive exact rationals, and there are no
identity-sensitive side constraints, groups, exact-cardinality conditions, or
mutual exclusions beyond the pure cover.  All incidence and weight
comparisons are exact.

\begin{proposition}[Exact preprocessing suite]
\label{prop:aor-preprocessing-feasibility-core}
Under the assumptions in the preceding paragraph, the following fixed-point
rules preserve feasibility, the exact optimum value including forced-weight
offsets, and at least one liftable optimum:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item fix the unique available carrier of any residual requirement;
 \item delete nonmandatory \(s\) when
       \(\mathcal R_s^Q\subset\mathcal R_t^Q\) and \(w_t\leq w_s\);
 \item for identical residual coverage, retain a stable minimum-weight
       representative and record every equal-weight substitution;
 \item after a forced selection, remove satisfied rows and recompute all
       rules;
 \item delete a nonmandatory column with empty residual coverage; and
 \item merge rows having identical carrier sets.
\end{enumerate}
Strictly cheaper dominance, strictly heavier duplicates, empty positive-weight
columns, forced columns, and duplicate rows preserve all original optimizer
identities.  Equal-weight duplicate columns recover all identities through the
recorded substitution map.  Equal-weight strict dominance need not preserve
all optimizer identities and makes no such claim.  Restarting the stable scan
after each transformation terminates after finitely many row or column
removals and yields a fixed point.  A zero-carrier residual row is an
infeasibility certificate, not an invitation to repair the instance.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.}
A unique carrier is forced by its covering row.  A no-heavier coverage
superset can replace a dominated selected column without losing a witness.
Identical columns permit exact substitution; identical rows impose the same
inequality; and an empty positive-weight column occurs in no optimum.  Forced
columns contribute a selection-independent offset, and propagation composes
these correspondences.  Every successful step removes a finite row or column,
which proves termination.  The strict/equal weight cases give precisely the
optimizer-identity qualifications in the statement.  Full propositions,
adversarial examples, reconstruction maps, and the deterministic rule order
are in Online Resource~1, Section~S4.
