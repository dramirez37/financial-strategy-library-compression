# AoOR claim boundary

Status: binding claim discipline for the journal program

Evidence cut: 2026-08-26

## Governing rule

The journal version may say only what the cited evidence class establishes.
Human proof, Lean verification, exact finite computation, floating-point
evidence, mixed-integer solver evidence, synthetic experiments, and
retrospective financial evidence are complementary but noninterchangeable.

## Evidence classes and permitted language

| Evidence class | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone | Required wording discipline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human-readable proof | A mathematical statement under its stated assumptions. | Lean kernel verification, correctness of code, or empirical relevance. | “We prove” only when the complete proof exists and the theorem ledger agrees. |
| Lean kernel verification | The exact encoded statement in the recorded Lean 4.32.0 environment. | Unencoded manuscript assumptions, implementation correctness, or empirical validity. | “Lean verifies the encoded theorem” with the ledger identifier; never “the paper is formally verified.” |
| Exact finite computation | A finite fixture, exhaustive oracle result, or exact equality in the declared instance. | A theorem beyond the exhausted domain or asymptotic performance. | “Exact finite computation confirms…” and state the finite domain. |
| Floating-point numerical evidence | Numerical behavior under the reported implementation, tolerances, and environment. | Exact equality, formal proof, or universal behavior. | “Numerical evidence indicates…” with tolerances and artifact provenance. |
| Mixed-integer solver evidence | The solver-reported status and returned candidate for the encoded model. | Formal or exhaustive proof of optimality; exact feasibility unless independently rechecked. | Report the solver status verbatim and separately report the exact postcheck. |
| Synthetic experimental evidence | Performance or structural patterns in a locked synthetic design. | Real-market performance, theoremhood, or conclusions outside the design. | “In the registered synthetic instances…” with design and seed provenance. |
| Retrospective financial evidence | Descriptive behavior in the two locked historical library audits. | Causality, forecasting, alpha, or deployable performance. | “In this retrospective audit…”; identify information timing and licensed-data boundary. |

## Exact algorithm-output certificate

Every returned library, whether produced by enumeration, DP, MILP,
preprocessing plus a solver, greedy construction, or deletion, must be checked
independently for:

1. exact equality of the operating frontier at every declared belief;
2. exact equality of the generative closure;
3. exact burden computed from the retained entries;
4. retention of the mandatory inactive strategy.

Passing these checks certifies feasibility and reported burden for that returned
library. It does not certify that a heuristic is optimal, that a solver's
optimality status is formally proved, or that two implementations share no
common defect. Small instances must additionally be compared with exact rational
oracles where practical.

## Mathematical boundaries

- The productive-state result is conditional on the declared finite
  partial-information process and its library-invariant primitives.
- Frontier and closure equality are source-relative. The result does not claim
  robustness to missing strategies, misspecified profiles, or an incorrect
  generator.
- The identity-closure tagged-cover model is a subclass. General closure may
  contain interactions not representable by per-strategy module tags.
- Belief obligations and module obligations are disjoint tags. Zero-frontier
  belief obligations are omitted because the mandatory inactive strategy
  supplies zero; active weights are positive in the intended complexity
  reduction.
- SC-GAP strengthens the fixed local/global witness only for its declared
  identity-closure family and the specifically defined rechecked
  heaviest-safe-first rule. A singleton-first maximal deletion sequence reaches
  the optimum, so no every-order lower bound may be inferred.
- Approximate compression is a distinct tolerance-based problem and cannot be
  used to support exact preservation claims.
- Scientific meanings of existing theorems are fixed. Shortening, renaming, or
  relocation may not weaken assumptions or strengthen conclusions.

If a proposed theorem is false, underspecified, or unproved, the work stops at a
documented issue. Experiments cannot be used to force the planned conclusion.

## Computational boundaries

- Julia work uses version 1.12.6 and the pinned project unless a documented
  reason requires otherwise.
- Lean work is limited to results selected in `THEOREM_LEDGER.md` and uses Lean
  4.32.0. Formalization is not added for appearance.
- Deterministic seeds, stable ordering, exact tie handling, and machine-readable
  schemas are part of the algorithm contract.
- The new algorithmic study, if authorized, must use
  `experiments/algorithmic_compression_v1/`, a new registry, new seeds, and a
  new design lock. The long final benchmark is run only after lock and explicit
  request.
- Runtime claims require recorded hardware/software context and committed raw
  benchmark summaries. Unobserved or interrupted runs are not results.
- Every manuscript number is generated from a committed artifact. Manual
  transcription is not an acceptable provenance path.

## Financial and licensed-data boundaries

- Raw CRSP/WRDS rows are neither accessed for this specification nor printed,
  committed, redistributed, or included in a submission bundle.
- Held-out financial information may be used only for ex-post evaluation. It
  may not enter pruning, weights, algorithm choice, hyperparameter choice, or
  optimization.
- The point-in-time portfolio-library study is retrospective and
  development-facing. It is not a prospective or independent confirmatory
  trading test.
- The calibrated closure-option experiment is synthetic mechanism evidence,
  not financial-panel evidence.
- The paper makes no causal claim, forecasting claim, alpha claim, or
  deployable-performance claim.
- Any new financial algorithm comparison requires a dated design lock that
  freezes inputs, weights, method variants, tie rules, statuses, failure
  handling, and estimands before computation.

## Artifact and release boundaries

- `release/v0.1.1-arxiv/` and the current preprint PDFs are immutable historical
  artifacts.
- The frozen randomized study and its committed result families are immutable.
- Existing artifact-manifest drift documented in `BASELINE_AUDIT.md` remains a
  release risk; this specification does not resolve it.
- Reorganization decisions in `MATERIAL_MIGRATION_MAP.md` do not authorize file
  deletion or movement. Historical sources remain recoverable and attributable.

## Submission-language boundary

The official audit found no full-manuscript length limit on the inspected
official pages. Planning ranges are internal targets, not journal requirements.
The journal version must eventually satisfy the verified abstract, keyword,
title-page, declaration, source, supplement, preprint, and accessibility rules
recorded in `requirements/OFFICIAL_REQUIREMENTS.md`. Any requirement still
marked for manual verification remains unclaimed until checked.

## Stop rules

Work on a contribution stops and records the reason when any of the following
occurs:

- the theorem statement is false, ambiguous, or lacks a complete proof;
- exact oracle and implementation disagree;
- postchecking fails any of the four invariants;
- a solver status is missing, inconsistent, or manually inferred;
- a design decision would use held-out financial information;
- a result number lacks a committed machine-readable source;
- completing the task would require modifying a frozen artifact.

The appropriate journal response is to narrow or remove the claim, not to fill
the gap with stronger prose.
