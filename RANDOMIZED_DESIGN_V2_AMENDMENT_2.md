# Randomized-library v2 amendment 2: executable raw-state protocol

## Status

This is a prospective implementation amendment made before evaluating any
registered v2 trial. It preserves:

- the fixed \(N=1024\);
- the complete \(2^7\) allocation and `trial_id` order;
- the master seed and all 4,096 recorded derived seeds;
- every factor, primitive level, estimand, precision checkpoint, and warning;
- the two existing design-lock hashes; and
- the frozen \(N=90\) pilot and all its artifacts.

No registered v2 library, action, value, loss, interaction sign, estimate,
table, report, or figure was generated before this amendment.

## State-dependent boundary-law adapter

The original protocol assigns 256 boundary trials the mechanism
`frontier_dependent_generator`. The legacy public generation overload receives
only a closure and therefore makes frontier dependence inexpressible. The
legacy rectangle audit also equates a state law with its closure-only overload.
Taken literally, those two interfaces would make the registered boundary
mechanism impossible while the raw design itself requires it.

This amendment supplies a v2-specific, more-specific method only for the
registered `V2StateGeneration` callable. For that callable:

- generation is evaluated from the actual compressed image of a raw library;
- admission and the joint completion law use that same actual state;
- raw and compressed transitions use the identical state-dependent law; and
- the rectangle audit requires project availability to equal the
  requirement-subset test on actual closure, while candidate and completion
  laws remain derived from the actual raw state.

Legacy process types and the frozen v1 runner retain their existing methods.
No compressed state, menu, transition, action value, or corner value is
accepted as an input.

## Exact raw generator

Every registered row deterministically creates:

- three beliefs;
- seven named modules;
- eight catalog strategies: inactive, three base strategies, one frontier
  addition, one closure carrier, and two descendants;
- the four commuting raw libraries \(L_{00},L_{01},L_{10},L_{11}\);
- the registered sparse/dense and low/high overlap templates;
- weak identity closure or strong two-trigger bridge closure;
- the exact cyclic persistence kernel;
- two requirement-derived projects with the registered cost, duration, and
  admission level;
- exact joint belief-path/admission completion laws; and
- a deletion order from the registered deletion seed.

The catalog, project, trial, and deletion streams use only their recorded
seeds. Profile jitter and descendant increments remain inside the original
registered supports.

`primitive_eligible` rows use a frontier-independent candidate. Boundary rows
use either the registered state-dependent candidate law or a project available
at poor closure with positive mechanically computed exposure. Assigned labels
never substitute for computed flags.

## Abort-first gates

All 1,024 trials are evaluated in memory before any registered output is
written. A failure raises an error and no artifact-writing phase begins.

For every trial the runner checks:

1. all process compressed states have an enumerated valid raw witness;
2. all four interaction corners are valid raw libraries from one catalog and
   closure;
3. commuting additions, edge identities, requirement-derived menus, raw
   transition pushforwards, and corner raw/compressed values agree;
4. raw and compressed finite-horizon values agree for every enumerated raw
   library, belief, and remaining horizon zero through four;
5. frontier-only pruning has zero passive-value loss;
6. innovation-safe pruning has exact zero frontier loss, closure loss,
   operational loss, generative loss, and total loss;
7. every method and single-asset signed loss decomposition closes exactly;
8. sparse/dense and overlap realization gates pass;
9. every theorem-condition component is evaluated from exact process laws and
   action tables;
10. intended eligible rows satisfy the computed predicate and intended
    boundary rows fail their registered component;
11. predicate-true interaction is nonpositive; and
12. theorem-facing scalars use `Rational{BigInt}`.

Counts and identifiers remain integers or strings. Decimal Wilson bounds,
MCSE square roots, and SVG coordinates are presentation-only transforms.

## Mechanically evaluated common-gap fields

At every belief and remaining horizon, the runner evaluates all feasible
actions at the four raw corners. It constructs a fixed positive low-frontier
recursive gap and zero high-frontier gap, derives each action's exact exposure
and shared intercept from its two frontier action values, and substitutes
those quantities back into both rational identities. It separately checks:

- frontier-independent menus;
- poor-to-rich menu inclusion;
- antitone recursive gap;
- nonnegative rich exposure;
- zero exposure for every poor feasible action; and
- both exact action-value identities.

This is a mechanical finite-row predicate, not a claim that arbitrary unified
models satisfy the primitive theorem assumptions.

## Outputs

All original v2 output paths remain unchanged. The amendment adds:

- `experiments/results/summaries/randomized_library_v2_interaction_signs.csv`,
  a denominator-explicit overall, predicate-conditional, and factor-level sign
  table; and
- `experiments/results/summaries/randomized_library_v2_raw_witness_manifest.csv`,
  one valid raw witness for every enumerated compressed state in every trial.

The corner, transition, pruning, asset, action, profile, module, closure,
kernel, and project tables are complete source/derivation data. The method,
factor, relationship, convergence, factor-stability, and interaction tables
are the underlying data for the report and four publication SVGs.

## Reproducibility boundary

The first successful run writes every registered output. A subsequent
`--check` reruns all 1,024 trials without mutation and requires byte identity.
Runtime does not enter a scientific artifact. These outputs remain
finite-generator simulation evidence only and do not prove or statistically
estimate a real-population claim.
