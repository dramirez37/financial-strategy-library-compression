\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Financial strategy libraries can be treated as productive inventories rather
than merely lists of currently attractive rules. Under the article's explicit
factorization assumption for a declared finite partial-information process,
the operating frontier and generative closure are a sufficient library
coordinate. Their exact equality then provides a precise source-relative
preservation contract. The result is conditional: if later transitions,
admission, duration, or rewards depend on raw provenance or identities beyond
those objects, the projection does not apply.

That semantic reduction leads to a distinct operations-research problem.
Under identity closure, safe compression is a tagged weighted cover; even a
restricted decision subclass is NP-complete. This complexity result does not
make set cover novel, nor does it extend the tagged formulation to arbitrary
closure. It explains why globally minimum retention burden cannot generally be
inferred from local deletion safety. The parameterized gap family sharpens
that distinction for a named heaviest-safe-first rule without claiming that
every deletion order fails.

The algorithmic response is deliberately plural. Enumeration and
requirement-mask dynamic programming provide independent exact finite methods
on small instances; exact preprocessing can expose forced structure; the MIP
workflow supplies auditable solver evidence; weighted greedy transfers a
classical covering guarantee within the identity-closure boundary; and
certified deletion methods provide feasible, irreducible endpoints without an
exactness claim. Every route ends with an original-instance certificate, so
feasibility does not depend on trusting a heuristic or solver status.

The registered synthetic benchmark supplies exact-method agreement,
certificate, heuristic-burden, failure, and preprocessing evidence. Its cold
Julia startup timings, unequal success targets, sparse structured grid, and
within-instance dependence do not support algorithm-speed rankings or general
scaling inference. These are locked finite-design findings under one
concurrent, dirty-worktree execution environment, not average-case complexity
or population evidence about financial libraries.

The retrospective point-in-time financial study illustrates the burden and
option implications for an explicit portfolio grammar. Innovation-safe
retention preserves every declared capability while materially reducing the
validation-computation index and exposing many proposal actions unavailable to
a budget-matched frontier-only library. Yet the protected policy never replaces
the common-equity comparator, and its one ETF replacement loses held-out value.
The calibrated synthetic experiment resolves the apparent tension: a small
positive bridge may be structurally present but statistically unrecoverable,
whereas a prospectively powered bridge is recovered under the declared noise
law. The first result is retrospective financial evidence; the second is
synthetic mechanism evidence. Neither supports a causal, forecasting, alpha,
or deployable-performance claim, and licensed source rows remain outside the
public artifact.

Two extensions are now most important. Algorithmically, nonidentity closure
requires a representation that preserves derived capability interactions
without replacing them by independent raw rows. Empirically, the next study
should use a newly locked market vintage or an independent panel, richer
documented institutional capability artifacts, and monetary maintenance costs
calibrated independently of postdecision outcomes. Neither extension can be obtained by relabeling
the present locked designs. Both should retain the separation among human
proof, formal verification, exact computation, solver evidence, synthetic
experiments, and retrospective financial evidence used here.
