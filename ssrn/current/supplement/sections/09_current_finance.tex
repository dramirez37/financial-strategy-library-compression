\section{Financial and mechanism methods}
\label{sec:supp-point-in-time-financial}

The point-in-time portfolio study is retrospective development evidence.
Its historical setting was known during design. Its sequential freezes protect
the stated within-study information boundary, without making the historical
sample an independent confirmatory test.

\subsection{Financial universe, strategy grammar, and timing}

For origin year \(y\), years \(y-5\) through \(y-3\) form the source library;
years \(y-2\) through \(y\) provide compression profiles; year \(y+1\) supplies
proposal estimates and freezes policy choices; year \(y+2\) evaluates those
choices. No evaluation value enters any choice. The primary universe contains
the 100 most liquid eligible US common equities at the origin. The separate
ETF replication selects up to 50 eligible plain ETFs and requires at least 20.
One ETF origin fails its universe gate and remains unavailable. Eligibility
uses predecision data and does not require subsequent survival.

The 96-specification grammar crosses three signals, two filters, two holding
horizons, two allocation rules, two gross exposures, and two exit rules.
Signals use 20- or 60-session momentum or five-session mean reversion; filters
are unrestricted entry or a 100-session trend filter. Allocation is equal
weight or inverse trailing volatility. Exposure is subject to the frozen
security cap and a 10\% annual portfolio-volatility ceiling; unused exposure
remains cash. Intended weights formed at close \(t\) first earn the return
indexed \(t+2\). Observed delisting returns are earned by the effective
position and followed by cash. A required missing return makes the path
unavailable; it is never imputed as zero. The current grammar and burden
registries fix the remaining rule values and identifiers.

Formation and compression each use 12 profile rows: three years crossed with
5- and 30-basis-point one-way costs and risk aversion 1 and 3. Each floating
profile is rounded to the nearest \(10^{-12}\) and represented as an exact
rational for comparison. Thus frontier equality is exact for the recorded
table, without treating measured returns as exact economic quantities.
The source contains all formation-frontier carriers plus the first three
formation-ranked carriers of each required capability. It is frozen before
compression profiles are computed. Each cell has
\AORPointInTimeCapabilities\ declared capabilities, including atomic rules,
interfaces, relevant point-in-time data, corporate actions, aggregation, and
cost/capacity support.

\subsection{Burden, compression, and proposal sets}

A strategy's burden is the exact integer sum of its components in the frozen
burden registry. It is a validation-computation index, with zero burden for
mandatory cash. The safe arm preserves the source frontier and full capability
union. The comparator first solves frontier-only retention and then adds whole
source entries in descending mean compression score, breaking ties by
identifier, whenever they fit within the safe arm's realized burden cap.
Every endpoint is independently checked in the unpreprocessed coordinates.
\AORPointInTimePreprocessingSolutions\ safe cells are resolved by exact
preprocessing; \AORPointInTimeSolverSolutions\ receive HiGHS
\texttt{OPTIMAL} status and an exact feasibility/burden postcheck.

Proposal actions comprise the grammar candidates excluded from the source
docket and enabled by the retained requirements. The comparator action set is
nested in the safe set before reading proposal scores. The complete candidate
ledger retains source-docket exclusions, missing capabilities, rejected
candidates, cash, and unavailable cells. The analysis unit is the origin;
candidate specifications and daily returns are not independent replications.

\subsection{Certainty equivalent and protected selection}

For gross daily portfolio return \(r_t\), one-way turnover \(q_t\), and
net return \(n_t=r_t-0.0005q_t\), the primary annual certainty equivalent is
\[
 \mathrm{CE}=252\,\overline r
 -252\,\overline{0.0005q}
 -\tfrac12\,3\,252\,\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(n),
\]
with the registered sample-variance convention. The mean, cost, and risk
penalty are reported separately. The economic adoption hurdle is 0.0025 in
annual CE units.

Proposal uncertainty uses 5,000 paired circular moving-block bootstrap draws
with 20-session blocks. A draw applies the same resampled session indices to
each candidate and baseline. If \(\delta_i\) is an estimated annual CE
difference and \(\mathrm{se}_i\) is the standard deviation of its bootstrap
differences, the reference maximum for each draw uses
\((\delta_i-\delta_i^*)/\mathrm{se}_i\), with zero used when the standard
error is zero. The 90th percentile of that maximum is the one-sided critical
value \(c\), giving lower bound \(\delta_i-c\,\mathrm{se}_i\).

The comparator selects cash unless an eligible candidate's simultaneous lower
bound over cash exceeds the hurdle. The safe policy defaults to that exact
choice. Because the comparator is selected from proposal data, the safe
reference family includes every complete safe-candidate by complete
comparator-candidate contrast, including cash. Replacement requires the
corresponding simultaneous lower bound to exceed the hurdle. Family shrinkage
ranks only candidates that pass this gate: its center is the family mean,
its latent variance is the nonnegative part of between-path sample variance
minus median squared standard error, and its reliability weights shrink each
candidate toward that center. Identifier order resolves remaining ties.

\subsection{Calibrated synthetic bridge experiment}

The two libraries have equal current frontier and burden; only the safe one
can run the specified descendant project. The parameters are
\(\beta=0.99\), \(\rho=0.98\), \(\pi=0.90\), and \(d=1\), so success
probability is 0.882. The net bridge margin is
\(M=\beta^d\rho^d\pi G-\kappa\). An oracle researches precisely when
\(M>0\); the learner researches when its noisy estimate exceeds an
independently calibrated threshold.

Calibration uses \AORPointInTimeCompletePaths\ complete financial proposal
paths and \AORPointInTimeResidualObservations\ residual observations, after
removing cross-strategy date means within cells. It estimates scale, lag-one
dependence, and tail thickness. Simulated proposal noise follows a stationary
AR(1) with variance-normalized Student innovations, 100 burn-in sessions,
756 proposal sessions, and annualization 252. A separate 4,096-world null
simulation fixes the threshold at the greater of the 0.0025 hurdle and its
95th percentile, giving 0.027008117603021155. Research cost and powered margin
are each twice that threshold.

The four margins are negative one threshold, zero, 0.0025, and positive two
thresholds. Each has 4,096 test worlds. Common random numbers pair regimes
within a world; calibration and test seeds are disjoint. Mean intervals use
mean plus or minus 1.96 standard errors; adoption uses Wilson intervals.
All four regimes and all worlds remain in the ledger. The primary
powered-oracle gate requires a positive lower endpoint and inclusion of the
analytic margin. The powered learner must have a positive lower endpoint and
at least 90\% adoption. Null and adverse point adoption must not exceed
6.5\% and 1\%, respectively.

The article reports the full four-regime table. Its null point adoption is
\AORClosureNullAdoption, while its Wilson upper endpoint is
\AORClosureNullWilsonUpper. The low positive dose does not yield a positive
learned interval; the powered one does. The powered margin is a designed dose,
and the experiment supports its declared synthetic mechanism rather than a
market premium. Structural assertions check frontier, burden, and bridge
availability in every world.

\subsection{Input access}

Public financial outputs contain aggregate results, structural counts, and
cryptographic seals. Raw CRSP/WRDS rows, security identifiers, return paths,
and selected identities derived from licensed returns are excluded. Full
source-data replication requires independent licensed access. Public aggregate
tables and the synthetic ledger have separate reproducible checks identified
in the reproduction guide.
