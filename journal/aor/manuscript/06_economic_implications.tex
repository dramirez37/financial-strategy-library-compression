\section{Economic consequences of unsafe and capacity-constrained retention}
\label{sec:economic-implications}
\label{sec:value}

The preservation constraint has an economic purpose beyond reproducing a
static payoff menu. Frontier equality protects current operating
opportunities; closure equality protects the inputs from which future
opportunities can be generated. This section first separates those two value
channels and then studies two outer resource decisions: retention under a hard
capacity and retention under a unit burden price. Neither outer problem
changes the generation, verification, admission, or positive-duration timing
inside the productive process of Section~\ref{sec:model-projection}.

\subsection{Operational and generative value}

For a finite horizon, let \(P_n(b,L)\) be the value when the library is frozen
and research is unavailable, and let \(U_n(b,L)\) be the corresponding full
value when every feasible positive-duration project is available. Thus

\begin{align}
 P_0(b,L)&=0,\nonumber\\
 P_{n+1}(b,L)&=F_L(b)+\beta
   \sum_{b'}P_B(b,b')P_n(b',L),
 \label{eq:passive-value}
\end{align}

where \(P_B\) is the filtered-belief transition under continued operation.
Write \(\Omega_n=U_n-P_n\) for the research-option premium. For inserting a
candidate \(s\), define total, operational, and generative increments by

\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_n(s\mid b,L)
   &:=U_n(b,L\cup\{s\})-U_n(b,L),\nonumber\\
 \Delta_n^{\mathrm{op}}(s\mid b,L)
   &:=P_n(b,L\cup\{s\})-P_n(b,L),\nonumber\\
 \Delta_n^{\mathrm{gen}}(s\mid b,L)
   &:=\Omega_n(b,L\cup\{s\})-\Omega_n(b,L).
 \label{eq:value-channels}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Operational--generative decomposition]
\label{thm:value-decomposition}
For every finite horizon \(n\), filtered belief \(b\), finite raw library
\(L\), and admissible candidate \(s\),

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_n(s\mid b,L)
 =\Delta_n^{\mathrm{op}}(s\mid b,L)
  +\Delta_n^{\mathrm{gen}}(s\mid b,L).
 \label{eq:value-decomposition}
\end{equation}

If \(F_{L\cup\{s\}}=F_L\), then the operational increment is zero. If in
addition \(C_{L\cup\{s\}}=C_L\), then the total increment is zero under the
projection assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:raw-to-compressed-projection}.
\end{proposition}

The equality is an accounting identity, not a sign theorem. A strategy may
be silent in the passive operating problem and valuable because it carries a
module that enables research; conversely, the generative residual need not be
positive if insertion changes costs or the relative appeal of projects. The
frontier--closure projection makes the two channels operationally useful:
frontier silence identifies zero current operating value, whereas joint
frontier and closure silence identifies zero productive value within the
declared process.

\subsection{Sharp loss from frontier-only retention}

Consider a dominated bridge strategy that uniquely carries the capability
needed for one descendant project. Its deletion leaves the source frontier
unchanged but makes the distinguished descendant unreachable. The following
statement fixes the scope of the loss calculation: the incumbent operating
block is common, there are no other differential projects or continuation
gains, and the terminal continuation gain is bounded by \(C\).

\begin{theorem}[Sharp normalized frontier-only bridge loss]
\label{thm:normalized-frontier-pruning-loss}
Fix exact rationals \(0\le\beta<1\), \(0\le\rho,\pi\le1\), a reward cap
\(C\ge0\), a project cost \(\kappa\ge0\), and an integer delay \(d\ge1\).
Suppose the bridge uniquely enables the descendant, retention gives survival
mass \(\rho^d\) and conditional admission probability \(\pi\), deletion makes
that descendant mass zero, and
\(\kappa\le\beta^d\rho^d\pi C\). Then the retained-minus-deleted value loss is

\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Loss}_d
 =\beta^d\rho^d\pi C-\kappa.
 \label{eq:normalized-pruning-loss}
\end{equation}

For fixed \((\beta,\rho,\pi,d,\kappa)\), this is the sharp upper bound over
descendant rewards in \([0,C]\), attained at \(C\). Whenever the denominator
is positive, the loss divided by the attainable net bridge value equals one.
If \(C\le1\), the additive loss is at most one; arbitrarily large additive
loss requires reward scaling.
\end{theorem}

The proof expands the one available project branch. Retention contributes
the discounted joint survival-and-admission mass times the capped continuation
gain, less cost; deletion contributes zero. Maximization over the reward
interval is attained at its upper endpoint. The factor
\(\rho^d\pi\) comes from sequential generation and conditional admission, not
from an independence assumption. Common operating value cancels under
frontier equality; if operation differs while research is in progress, its
exact discounted difference must be added. Online Resource~1 gives the full
fixture, proof, and delay qualifications.

The result explains why operational redundancy is not generative redundancy.
A frontier-only rule can be exact for today's operating menu and still remove
the entire positive net value of a future bridge. It does not assert an
unbounded normalized loss or a general value formula for arbitrary deleted
libraries.

\subsection{Hard retention capacity and complementarity}

Let \(\mathfrak L_\theta\) be a nonempty finite family of outer-certified
libraries, let \(W(L')\ge0\) be retention burden, and let \(V_\theta(b,L')\)
be productive value under the unchanged inner process. Capacity selection is

\begin{equation}
 V_\theta^\star(b;B)
 :=\max_{L'\in\mathfrak L_\theta:\,W(L')\le B}V_\theta(b,L').
 \label{eq:capacity-value}
\end{equation}

Unlike exact source-relative compression, this problem permits a productive
trade-off when capacity is insufficient to preserve a designated source.

\begin{theorem}[Finite hard-capacity retention]
\label{thm:capacity-value}
Suppose \(\mathfrak L_\theta\) is finite and contains the zero-burden
inactive-only library. For every \(B\ge0\), the maximum in
\eqref{eq:capacity-value} is attained. The function
\(B\mapsto V_\theta^\star(b;B)\) is nondecreasing, is constant between
consecutive attainable burdens, and can change only at an attainable burden.
Every exact forward capacity increment is nonnegative. Neither uniqueness
nor inclusion nesting of maximizing libraries follows.
\end{theorem}

The proof is finite maximization plus set inclusion of the feasible families
as \(B\) rises. Discreteness matters. With two unit-burden capabilities that
produce no value separately and value one jointly, successive attainable
values are \((0,0,1)\). The capacity function is therefore not generally
concave, and marginal capacity increments need not diminish. A hard-capacity
optimizer can also be unsupported by every scalar resource price because a
finite nonconcave point need not lie on the upper convex envelope selected by
penalization.

\subsection{Resource-price envelope}

For a nonnegative unit price \(\lambda\), the corresponding outer penalized
problem is

\begin{equation}
 J_\theta^\star(b;\lambda)
 :=\max_{L'\in\mathfrak L_\theta}
 \{V_\theta(b,L')-\lambda W(L')\}.
 \label{eq:penalized-value}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite resource-price envelope]
\label{thm:penalized-envelope}
For a fixed nonempty finite eligible family, the maximum in
\eqref{eq:penalized-value} is attained. The optimized value is continuous,
nonincreasing, convex, and piecewise affine in the real price \(\lambda\ge0\),
with switching candidates among finitely many pairwise branch intersections.
If \(0\le\lambda_1<\lambda_2\), every optimizer at \(\lambda_2\) has weakly
lower burden than every optimizer at \(\lambda_1\). Wherever one library is
strictly dominant, the local slope is \(-W(L')\).
\end{theorem}

Each eligible library supplies the affine branch
\(V_\theta(b,L')-\lambda W(L')\), so the conclusions follow from the upper
envelope. For two unequal-burden libraries the pairwise candidate crossing
price is their value difference divided by their burden difference, but it is
an actual breakpoint only when both branches are envelope-active. At a tie
the optimizer is set-valued. The theorem orders optimizer burdens across
prices; it does not imply that their raw identities are nested.
