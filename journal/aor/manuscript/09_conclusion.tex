\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Financial strategy libraries are productive inventories, not merely lists of
currently attractive rules. In the declared finite partial-information
process, their operating frontier and generative closure are the library
component of the sufficient state. Exact equality of those objects therefore
provides a precise source-relative preservation contract: a retained library
supports the same current operating menu and the same capabilities for future
generation, even when its raw identities differ.

That semantic reduction leads to a distinct operations-research problem.
Under identity closure, safe compression is a tagged weighted cover; even a
restricted decision subclass is NP-complete. This complexity result does not
make set cover novel, nor does it extend the tagged formulation to arbitrary
closure. It explains why globally minimum retention burden cannot generally be
inferred from local deletion safety. The parameterized gap family sharpens
that distinction for a named heaviest-safe-first rule without claiming that
every deletion order fails.

The algorithmic response is deliberately plural. Enumeration and
requirement-mask dynamic programming provide independent exact finite methods
on small instances; exact preprocessing can expose forced structure; the MIP
workflow supplies auditable solver evidence; weighted greedy transfers a
classical covering guarantee within the identity-closure boundary; and
certified deletion methods provide feasible, irreducible endpoints without an
exactness claim. Every route ends with an original-instance certificate, so
feasibility does not depend on trusting a heuristic or solver status.

The registered synthetic benchmark locates a computational boundary rather
than declaring a universal winner. Exact methods agree where all required
comparisons are available, but heuristic burdens and runtime depend on
algorithm and structure. Full preprocessing can collapse a model while still
adding end-to-end overhead, and multi-start search can improve a first
deletion order while remaining exposed to memory limits. These are locked
finite-design findings under one concurrent hardware environment, not
average-case complexity or population evidence about financial libraries.

The retrospective financial cases show the resource implication. Exact
frontier and closure preservation permits substantial compression, and global
weight-aware selection can improve on an already safe historical endpoint.
The ranking and identities depend on the prespecified burden schedule, which
makes governance design part of the optimization specification. Held-out
opportunity quantities are postdecision diagnostics only; the analysis makes
no causal, forecasting, alpha, or deployable-performance claim, and licensed
source rows remain outside the public artifact.

The most important extension is algorithmic treatment of nonidentity closure.
Closure complementarities can invalidate independent module rows, so progress
requires a representation that preserves derived capability interactions
without silently replacing them by raw coverage. A useful next step would pair
such a representation with exact certificates and prospectively registered
scaling evidence, retaining the same separation among human proof, formal
verification, exact computation, solver evidence, synthetic experiments, and
retrospective financial evidence used here.
