\section{Source-by-source four-claim boundary audit}
\label{supp:literature-novelty-audit}

This supplement preserves the granular comparison removed from the main
literature review.  The audit asks whether each source entails any of the
paper's four joint finite-model claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{C1.}] minimum resource burden among raw sublibraries preserving
  both the current operating frontier and the future descendant-generating
  closure;
\item[\textbf{C2.}] process-safe deletion if and only if both frontier and
  closure are preserved, under closure detectability for necessity;
\item[\textbf{C3.}] a sharp normalized dynamic loss from retaining the
  frontier while dropping the closure constraint; and
\item[\textbf{C4.}] capacity and resource-price optimized value envelopes
  together with operational and generative value-channel elasticities.
\end{enumerate}
The audit concedes broad overlap and makes no priority inference from an
absence.  ``Does not entail'' means only that the cited result does not prove
the stated claim under this paper's raw generation, verification, admission,
and deletion semantics.

\subsection{Partial-information and semi-Markov control}

\paragraph{Li, Nystr\"om, and Olofsson; Dai, Zhang, and Zhu.}
Partial-information switching and hidden-regime trading derive filtered
control formulations, numerical schemes, and posterior-indexed policy regions
\cite{LiNystromOlofsson2015,DaiZhangZhu2010}.  The control menu is fixed: a
retention decision changes neither future candidates nor their admission.
Consequently the models have no raw sublibrary resource problem (C1),
frontier--closure deletion test (C2), closure-relaxation loss (C3), or
dual-channel optimized library value (C4).

\paragraph{White.}
The partially observed semi-Markov model gives dynamic programming on a
sufficient statistic, concavity, and finite-control piecewise linearity
\cite{White1976}.  It is the closest timing and belief-state precedent, but
its fixed control set has no retained generative parents.  It therefore
supplies the control architecture but none of C1--C4.

\subsection{POMDP reduction, policy compression, and runtime safety}

\paragraph{Smallwood and Sondik; Cassandra, Littman, and Zhang.}
Alpha-vector envelopes and incremental pruning preserve a fixed POMDP value
backup \cite{SmallwoodSondik1973,CassandraLittmanZhang1997}.  Their vectors do
not carry modules used to generate later policies.  The results establish
neither minimum resource retention of raw parents (C1) nor a process-level
frontier--closure iff (C2); without a deleted carrier they imply neither C3
nor C4.

\paragraph{Givan, Dean, and Greig.}
Stochastic bisimulation gives behavior-preserving state equivalence and model
minimization within a specified comparison class \cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}.
It is generic reduction machinery, not a derivation of this model's raw
frontier--closure statistic.  Applied to a fully encoded innovation model it
could support reduction, but it does not itself formulate C1--C4.

\paragraph{Mutti, Del Col, and Restelli.}
Reward-free policy-space compression constructs finite representatives with
R\'enyi-divergence coverage guarantees and an NP-hard set-cover formulation
\cite{MuttiDelColRestelli2022}.  This directly overlaps with compact policy
sets and static coverage.  Representatives do not generate descendants, so
the preserved object differs from C1 and C2; the paper has no
carrier-deletion witness for C3 and no operational--generative envelope or
elasticity result for C4.

\paragraph{Sim\~ao, Suilen, and Jansen.}
Safe policy improvement for POMDPs uses behavior-controller memory and offline
data to improve one finite-state controller with a high-probability guarantee
\cite{SimaoSuilenJansen2023}.  It overlaps with partial information and
verification-aware admission, but contains no evolving parent library,
resource-minimal subset, deletion semantics, closure loss, or dual-channel
library value; hence none of C1--C4 follows.

\paragraph{Kim et al.}
Policy Library CBF gives a sufficient language-metric coverage condition for
finite-horizon runtime safety using a static fallback-policy library
\cite{KimEtAl2026PLCBF}.  Runtime certification is a preservation objective,
but the library is nongenerative.  Static safety coverage is not C1 or C2,
and no deletion-induced descendant channel appears in C3 or C4.

\subsection{Resource selection and generative action sets}

\paragraph{Dey et al.}
Efficient optimization of control libraries selects and orders compact
maneuver or trajectory libraries using submodular-sequence structure and
approximation guarantees \cite{DeyEtAl2012}.  This is direct overlap with a
resource-valued library decision.  Its objective evaluates static control
coverage or usefulness, not simultaneous preservation of current operation
and parent-conditioned descendants (C1--C2).  It contains neither the
closure-relaxation construction (C3) nor the optimized dual-channel value and
elasticity system (C4).

\paragraph{Russell and Wefald; Hay et al.}
Metareasoning and computation selection value costly computation by its effect
on a later decision \cite{RussellWefald1991,HayEtAl2012}.  Research cost and
option value are therefore prior ideas.  The decision object is a computation,
not a compressed library of executable generative parents, so these sources
do not formulate C1--C3 or the particular optimized envelope and channel
elasticities in C4.

\paragraph{Golovin and Krause.}
Adaptive submodularity provides greedy guarantees under adaptive monotonicity
and diminishing returns \cite{GolovinKrause2011}.  It is relevant to
resource-constrained stochastic selection, but the present generator can
create complementarities and locally irreducible nonminimal libraries.  The
general theory does not entail the paper's preservation constraint (C1--C2),
loss witness (C3), or finite optimized value system (C4).

\paragraph{Xu et al.}
Generative Action Sets lets an online agent pay once to create a permanently
reusable action and proves an optimal sublinear regret rate for balancing
creation, exploration, and reuse \cite{XuEtAl2026GenerativeActions}.  This
establishes costly endogenous action expansion.  Its generation oracle is not
conditioned on which retained parents survive, so there is no deletion-based
resource minimum or iff (C1--C2), no carrier loss (C3), and a regret objective
rather than C4's optimized operational--generative value.

\paragraph{Mayorqu\'in Posadas and Vega.}
The B* expansion system iteratively proposes, validates, and retains reusable
actions in a fully observable environment and reports empirical task gains
\cite{MayorquinPosadasVega2026}.  It overlaps with validated menu expansion,
but has neither parent-conditioned deletion nor partial-information library
value, and thus does not entail C1--C4.

\subsection{Maintained skill libraries}

\paragraph{Shen et al.: SkillFoundry.}
SkillFoundry builds validated executable scientific skill packages and uses
closed-loop tests to expand, repair, merge, and prune a persistent library
\cite{ShenEtAl2026SkillFoundry}.  It is the closest end-to-end validated
library lifecycle.  Its maintenance objective is procedural and empirical:
it does not minimize burden subject to semantic frontier--closure preservation
(C1), prove the deletion iff (C2), quantify normalized closure loss (C3), or
derive C4.

\paragraph{Pu, Song, and Zhao: SkillOps.}
SkillOps represents typed contracts and dependency or compatibility links,
then combines utility, risk, compatibility, and validator signals in merge,
repair, retirement, adaptation, and validator-insertion rules
\cite{PuSongZhao2026SkillOps}.  It makes explicit that a local change can alter
later retrieval, composition, and execution.  The ecosystem graph is not a
probabilistic descendant closure, however, and its rule-based maintenance
does not establish C1--C4.

\paragraph{Shi, Yuan, and Liu: Programmatic Skill Networks.}
Executable symbolic programs and invocation links support planning and new
skill generation; maintenance includes duplicate removal, link redirection,
branch pruning, and recent-success rollback validation
\cite{ShiYuanLiu2026PSN}.  This is the closest system-level overlap with
executable parents plus validated refactoring.  Finite-task validation does
not certify every current frontier action and every feasible future admitted
descendant.  The source therefore does not entail the global resource problem
(C1), the semantic iff (C2), the normalized carrier loss (C3), or the
optimized value and elasticity result (C4).

\paragraph{Xia et al.: SkillRL.}
A hierarchical SkillBank and agent policy co-evolve through recursive
reinforcement learning \cite{XiaEtAl2026SkillRL}.  This establishes that a
library can shape later learning, but there is no executable-policy frontier,
all-descendant closure, certified deletion, or resource/value theorem; none
of C1--C4 follows.

\paragraph{Li et al.: SkillGraph.}
Graph-linked skills encode prerequisite, enhancement, and co-occurrence
relations and guide composition, merge, split, deprecation, or retrieval
removal \cite{LiEtAl2026SkillGraph}.  Dependency-aware retirement is direct
conceptual overlap.  Structural heuristics do not prove preservation of all
current controls and descendants, and the system has no counterparts to C1,
C3, or C4.

\paragraph{Wang et al.: AutoSkills.}
AutoSkills constructs and refines hierarchical strategic, functional, and
atomic skills with validated exploratory expansion
\cite{WangEtAl2026AutoSkills}.  Automated persistent library construction is
prior art, but the source states no minimum-resource preservation, semantic
deletion iff, normalized dynamic loss, or value-envelope theorem.

\paragraph{Pan et al.: Anything2Skill.}
Structured skill contracts, a taxonomy, and a versioned SkillBank support
creation, revision, merge, split, deprecation, and discard
\cite{PanEtAl2026Anything2Skill}.  This is provenance-aware lifecycle
maintenance.  Its operations have no certificate preserving current control
and every future descendant, so they imply none of C1--C4.

\paragraph{Hernandez Cano et al.}
Prospective Compression selects reusable helper abstractions under uncertainty
about future tasks and gives a computational complexity result
\cite{HernandezCanoEtAl2026}.  It directly values future generation rather
than retrospective fit.  Helpers are not simultaneously executable controls
in a belief-state admission process, so the formal objective is distinct from
C1--C2 and supplies neither C3 nor C4.

\paragraph{Chun et al.: Functional Cache Grafting.}
Validated cached function skeletons are retrieved, stitched, and patched into
composite embodied code policies \cite{ChunEtAl2026FCGraft}.  The source
shows policies built from reusable validated components but has no evolving
library deletion semantics, belief-state resource problem, normalized loss,
or dual-channel optimized value; it does not entail C1--C4.

\paragraph{Song and Wei: Skill Shadowing.}
Skill Shadowing decomposes the pass-rate decline from expanding a skill
library into context overhead and selection shadowing and gives component
bounds \cite{SongWei2026SkillShadowing}.  This is an exact static library-loss
decomposition, but not deletion of a generative carrier.  It entails neither
C1--C2 nor C3's delayed bridge loss, and its decomposition is not C4's
operational--generative insertion value and elasticity system.

\subsection{Generated financial strategies}

\paragraph{Allen and Karjalainen; Moody and Saffell.}
Genetic search for technical trading rules and direct reinforcement learning
for trading policies establish automated financial-strategy construction
\cite{AllenKarjalainen1999,MoodySaffell2001}.  They do not maintain a
resource-minimized persistent parent library, so none of C1--C4 is implied.

\paragraph{Wang et al.: FactorMiner.}
Formulaic alpha factors, evaluation skills, and experience memory guide later
factor generation, while information-coefficient and correlation screens
support admission and replacement \cite{WangEtAl2026FactorMiner}.  This is
the closest financial self-evolving library with explicit screening.  Factors
are not belief-dependent executable parents and memory is not a formal
descendant closure, so the source does not establish C1--C4.

\paragraph{Pan et al.: ReCAP.}
Regime-specific portfolio-policy vectors are composed through a regime gate
and maintained with similarity, attention, and norm-based merge or discard
rules \cite{PanEtAl2026ReCAP}.  It directly overlaps with financial
policy-library compression, but the policies do not parent later generated
strategies.  The source therefore has no closure-preserving minimum or iff
(C1--C2), descendant-loss construction (C3), or dual-channel optimized value
(C4).

\paragraph{Sharma and Shroff: AlgoEvolve.}
Executable trading programs and synthesis-prompt genomes evolve in nested
loops; selected programs parent later mutations and walk-forward performance
supplies the fitness signal \cite{SharmaShroff2026AlgoEvolve}.  This is direct
overlap with strategies that both execute and generate variants.  Population
selection is not semantic compression of a persistent admitted library under
partial belief, and the system reports no results matching C1--C4.

\paragraph{Mao et al.: EvoQuant.}
Typed strategy genomes, provenance, controlled edits, distilled experience,
hard gates, out-of-sample checks, and adopt/incubate/reject decisions define a
verifier-guided optimization system \cite{MaoEtAl2026EvoQuant}.  It evolves
an incumbent and its history rather than a retained parent library.  It hence
does not entail minimum-resource frontier--closure preservation (C1), a
library deletion iff (C2), normalized deletion loss (C3), or C4's optimized
library-value and elasticity statements.

\paragraph{Audit conclusion.}
The closest sources establish belief-state control, fixed-model reduction,
static or reward-free library coverage, compact control-library selection,
costly action creation, dependency-aware skill maintenance, executable parent
programs, and verifier-gated financial evolution.  Those ingredients are
prior art.  The claimed boundary is their joint use in C1--C4 under one finite
raw library law; the manuscript makes no broader claim to have introduced
compression, pruning, evolving libraries, generated actions, or automated
trading strategies.
