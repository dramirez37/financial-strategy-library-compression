\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Financial research is cumulative. Candidate signals, transformations,
execution rules, and risk controls enter maintained catalogs after development
and validation, where they remain available for operation, comparison, and
reuse. As a catalog grows, so do its computing footprint, validation and
monitoring queue, documentation burden, and the attention needed to understand
it. The decision studied here is removal from an \emph{active maintenance and
validation set}. It is not destruction of code, data, model history, or records
whose archival retention is required by law, contract, audit policy, or
disaster-recovery practice.

The apparent redundancy of a strategy depends on which role is being assessed.
For current operation, a strategy is redundant if removing it leaves every
regime-dependent operating opportunity unchanged. For future research, the
same strategy may be the only declared carrier of a signal, transformation,
execution component, or risk-control tag used by a later project. A
strategy can consequently be dominated at every current belief and still be
generatively indispensable. Pruning only the current payoff envelope can
preserve what the library can operate now while silently changing what it can
help create later. This carrier interpretation is a modeling declaration: in
the financial cases the tags describe a fixed tuple grammar, not evidence that
the underlying code, data, tests, or organizational knowledge exist only in a
strategy row.

We represent these roles by two source-library objects. The operating frontier
\(F_L\) records the best current operating value attainable from a source
library \(L\) at each declared belief. The generative closure \(C_L\) records
the modules available to the modeled strategy-generation process after its
closure rules have been applied. Conditional on the paper's finite
library-invariance assumptions, the pair \((F_L,C_L)\) is the library component
of a controlled representation: replacing \(L\) by a sublibrary with the same
frontier and closure leaves the declared operating opportunities and
generative menus unchanged. The theorem is a factorization result for a
specified information architecture, not a generic coarsest-state or minimality
theorem. The filtered belief and any active-project variables remain part of
the full controlled state.

This semantic reduction defines \emph{innovation-safe compression}. Given a
source library, additive retention burdens, and a mandatory inactive strategy,
we seek a minimum-burden source sublibrary whose frontier and closure equal
those of the source exactly. Equality is source-relative and exact for the
declared finite tables: it is not a prediction criterion, statistical
certainty, or a claim of equivalence in unobserved markets. The source itself
is feasible, but choosing the least-burden
feasible representation is a global combinatorial optimization problem.
Sequential deletion can certify that every accepted step is safe, yet an
inclusion-wise irreducible endpoint need not have minimum burden.

The identity-closure subclass exposes the combinatorial structure sharply. In
that subclass, the requirements are tagged to keep frontier-attainment rows
distinct from module-carrier rows, including frontier ties and requirements
already met by the inactive strategy. A sublibrary is safe if and only if its
strategies cover every remaining tagged requirement. This equivalence reduces
the semantic retention problem to a weighted covering formulation; it does not
claim set cover itself as a new problem. The restricted decision problem is
NP-complete, and the broader compression model is NP-hard because it contains
that subclass. The reduction therefore supplies both a modeling bridge to the
classical covering literature \citep{Karp1972,Chvatal1979} and a reason not to
expect local redundancy checks alone to solve global resource allocation.

We address this boundary with a portfolio of algorithms rather than a single
method. Complete enumeration provides a small-library oracle.
Requirement-mask dynamic programming is exact when the tagged requirement
dimension is manageable. Proven preprocessing rules force unique carriers,
remove safely dominated or empty variables, merge duplicate coverage and
requirement patterns with explicit reconstruction maps, and can sometimes
resolve an instance completely. An auditable mixed-integer formulation scales
the covering model while retaining solver diagnostics and an independent exact
postcheck of the returned library. Weighted greedy construction inherits its
classical covering guarantee under identity closure, and reverse deletion can
reduce its burden without weakening that guarantee. Deterministic and
multi-start deletion heuristics provide certified feasible endpoints, but
their feasibility certificates are not certificates of optimality.

The computational evidence asks a narrower question: how do certified burdens
differ within one registered synthetic design? Small instances support exact
cross-method comparisons, and the adversarial family connects finite
calculation to the worst-case theorem. The structured generator frequently
collapses under forced preprocessing and contains infeasible requested factor
cells. Moreover, recorded wall times include first-call Julia compilation
under concurrent worker contention. We therefore use the study for exact
agreement, burden quality, failure accounting, and preprocessing prevalence,
not for steady-state runtime rankings, complete factorial inference, or a
population claim about financial libraries.

The application evidence consists of separate retrospective terminal and
annual financial tuple-grammar audits. They compare safe algorithms under
predeclared burden indices and recheck mandatory retention, frontier equality,
closure equality, and burden. Both residual cover models are resolved by exact
preprocessing, so the cases illustrate semantics and burden accounting rather
than difficult financial optimization. Financial quantities from later audit
periods do not enter weights, preprocessing, algorithm choice, warm starts,
pruning order, or optimization; the resulting ex post catalog witness is read
only after selection. Terminal and annual units remain separate. The cases do
not establish population savings, causality, forecasting skill, alpha, or
deployable performance.

\subsection{Research questions}

The analysis is organized around four questions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{RQ1.}] Under which declared library-invariance conditions does
frontier--closure equality preserve both current operating opportunities and
the modeled strategy-generation menu?

\item[\textbf{RQ2.}] How does exact minimum-burden preservation become a
covering problem under identity closure, what computational barrier follows,
and how far can a certified local-deletion rule be from the global optimum?

\item[\textbf{RQ3.}] Which exact, approximation-guaranteed, mixed-integer, and
certified heuristic methods can exploit this structure while preserving exact
feasibility and an auditable reconstruction to original strategy identities?

\item[\textbf{RQ4.}] Within the registered synthetic design and two
retrospective financial cases, when do certified heuristics match an available
reference burden, and when do safe endpoints differ in burden or identities?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}

The paper makes four principal contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Conditional semantic representation.} We identify operationally
checkable library-invariance conditions under which the operating frontier and
generative closure form a sufficient library summary, and we formulate
innovation-safe compression as source-relative preservation of both objects.
This separates operational from generative redundancy without claiming a
generic minimal state.

\item \emph{Complexity and worst-case theory.} For identity closure, we prove
the tagged-cover equivalence and NP-completeness of a restricted safe-compression
decision problem. A parameterized family gives the exact burden ratio of the
heaviest-safe-first endpoint to the global optimum and shows that this named
deletion rule has no constant approximation guarantee. The result does not
apply to every deletion order.

\item \emph{Certified computational reduction.} We map the semantic constraint
to established weighted-cover machinery under identity closure and provide a
common exact representation, optimum-preserving preprocessing with
reconstruction, enumeration, requirement-mask dynamic programming, an
auditable mixed-integer workflow, weighted greedy, and certified deletion.
These are implementation and certification contributions, not claims that the
underlying generic cover algorithms are new.

\item \emph{Bounded computational and financial evidence.} The locked
benchmark supplies exact-agreement, certified burden-quality, preprocessing,
and adversarial records within its realized design. Two retrospective financial
cases report table-exact preservation and burden-index sensitivity while
preserving licensed-data and postdecision-information boundaries. Neither
layer is generalized beyond its registered instances.
\end{enumerate}

The theoretical, computational, solver, and financial evidence levels are not
interchangeable. Universal claims rest on their stated human proofs and, where
the theorem ledger says so, narrower kernel-checked declarations. Exact finite
computation establishes facts about specified fixtures or instances. Solver
diagnostics document a numerical global-search process, whereas independent
exact rechecks certify the feasibility and burden of the returned sublibrary.
Registered synthetic results describe the locked generators, and retrospective
financial results describe the audited source instances. The algorithmic
guarantees in this article apply to identity closure unless a result explicitly
states otherwise; we do not claim a covering algorithm for arbitrary
nonidentity closure.
