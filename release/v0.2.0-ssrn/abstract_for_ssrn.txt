Financial strategy libraries impose maintenance, validation, monitoring, governance, computing, and attention burdens. An entry that is redundant for current operation may nevertheless carry a capability used by a later strategy-generation process. We formulate source-relative minimum-burden retention that preserves both a regime-indexed operating frontier and a declared generative closure. Under explicit library-invariance assumptions, these objects form the library component of a controlled finite-state representation; this is a conditional semantic result, not a general minimality claim. Under identity closure, safe retention is exactly weighted set cover on disjoint frontier and module tags. This established combinatorial class yields a restricted NP-completeness boundary and supports enumeration, requirement-mask dynamic programming, exact preprocessing, mixed-integer optimization, weighted greedy construction, and certified deletion. A parameterized family proves that heaviest-safe-first deletion has no constant approximation guarantee. A registered and audited synthetic benchmark verifies exact-method agreement where applicable, reports certified burden gaps, and shows that preprocessing frequently resolves the generated instances; its cold-start concurrent timings are not used to rank algorithms. A retrospective point-in-time portfolio-library study finds large burden-index reductions and many additional innovation options, but its protected common-equity policy never exercises those options and its single ETF replacement loses held-out value. A separately design-locked synthetic mechanism experiment shows why option preservation and option exercise differ: the learner does not recover a small positive bridge value but does recover a prospectively powered one. These are bounded structural, synthetic, and retrospective findings, not evidence of alpha, forecasting ability, causal effects, or deployable performance.
