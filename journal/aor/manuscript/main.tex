\abstract{Financial strategy libraries accumulate storage, validation,
monitoring, governance, computing, and human-attention burdens. Yet an entry
that is redundant for current operation may remain essential as a carrier of
capabilities used to generate future strategies. We study source-relative,
minimum-cost retention under a finite partial-information research process.
The operating frontier and generative closure form the library component of
the productive state, so innovation-safe compression requires exact
preservation of both objects and retention of the inactive strategy. Under
identity closure, this condition is equivalent to covering a tagged universe
of frontier-attainment and module requirements. The associated restricted
decision problem is complete for nondeterministic polynomial time
(NP-complete). We develop exact enumeration and
requirement-mask dynamic programming, exact preprocessing, an auditable
mixed-integer formulation, a weighted greedy construction with a transferred
set-cover guarantee, and certified deletion heuristics. A parameterized family
shows that heaviest-safe-first deletion has no constant approximation
guarantee. A prospectively registered and audited computational benchmark
distinguishes settings in which heuristics attain exact or certified reference
burdens from settings in which method choice changes retention burden.
Retrospective terminal and annual walk-forward financial audits quantify
burden savings under predeclared retention-weight schedules, with
exact frontier and closure rechecks. Held-out financial diagnostics are
evaluated only after retention decisions. The evidence is computational and
retrospective: it supports no causal, forecasting, alpha, or
deployable-performance claim.}

\keywords{financial strategy libraries, innovation-safe compression, weighted
set cover, partial information, exact algorithms, model governance}
